The cancellation window

What the market research actually found, where VIGIL genuinely stands, and the holes that matter more than the next feature.

The position

Everyone else sells speed. Sell not having to race.

Every competing product in this market runs the same hook: never miss a drop, before it sells out, never overpay. All of them frame the problem the same way — you were too slow.

Not one frames it as the race itself being the problem. That's the opening, and it happens to be the only claim VIGIL can honestly make: it polls at human rates with jitter and cannot win a millisecond race. What it has isn't speed. It's attention — it's watching at 3am, and you aren't.

The seat wasn't gone. You just weren't looking at the right second.
That was never a “sold out” problem — it's a timing problem.

Why the problem is real

Cancellations cluster exactly where a human can't reach

A competitor published an analysis of 5,600+ tracked seat openings. It is the strongest single piece of evidence for this entire product category:

11.7h
median lead time between a seat freeing up and the showtime — with 68% inside 24 hours, and roughly one in nine in the final hour before the film starts.

That is the whole argument. The seats come back overnight, mid-workday, in the last hour — a distribution no person can cover by refreshing, and one that rewards whoever happens to be looking at that minute.

The demand signal is not in doubt elsewhere either: the BFI IMAX sold out all 173 of its 70mm Odyssey screenings, with new batches gone in under 30 minutes against 40,000-deep queues. One solo engineer's seat-watcher reached 5,000+ users in weeks. Another wired up a talking robot to announce openings, because nothing existed that did it. People building robots is what unmet demand looks like.

The uncomfortable part

VIGIL is not first, and planning as if it were would be the mistake

This is the finding that most changes the plan. The US cinema seat-alert niche is already occupied — including by a free incumbent.

So “we watch and never buy” is not a differentiator on its own. What is still unclaimed is making that refusal architectural and verifiable — no stored credentials, no cart integration, no checkout step, provable in open source — rather than a policy line in a Discord rulebook.

Where the ground is actually open

Four gaps survived scrutiny

Open
Europe — the strongest wedge Every cinema seat-alert tool found is US or Canada only. Zero coverage of Odeon, Cineworld, Vue, Picturehouse, Everyman or BFI IMAX — and there is a literal Odeon App Store review asking for this. VIGIL's catalogue already covers 87 chains across 59 countries, heavily European. Do not open by fighting a free US incumbent on its own ground.
Open
Consumer-side Discord delivery Cook groups charge $30–95/month substantially for the service of delivering alerts into Discord. The only non-broker product found with a Discord webhook targets ticket resellers. Already built here — free, client-fired, no server.
Open
“It's available” fused with “it's worth it” Restock alerting and price/value data live in entirely separate products across every vertical examined. VIGIL already stores rolling price history for its median calculation.
Contested
The latency band Generic change-detection tools floor at 2 minutes even at $99/mo. Cook groups deliver in 60–90 seconds for $75/mo. There is an empty band between them — but competing on latency means competing on the axis VIGIL deliberately refuses to optimise.
Taken
“Bot-free” as a slogan Claimed already, by more than one competitor. Only the verifiable version is still available.

The holes

What's actually broken or missing

Ranked by how much they threaten the product, not by how hard they are to fix.

It has never touched a real website

Every test is a fixture written here. The 87-chain catalogue is unverified against live sites. The shared-template folder is empty. Nobody has recorded a single real watch on a single real cinema. Seven audits have now found bugs that only synthetic data could hide — and that will keep being true until it meets one real booking page. This is the highest-value thing anyone can do next, and it needs a human with a real logged-in session.

Live seat maps can't be watched at all

High-demand venues with live seat-locking push state over a WebSocket rather than a request — the flagship use case is the most likely place to hit this. There is no request to replay, and a background worker that dies after 30 seconds idle can't hold a connection open. Now at least detected and explained honestly instead of blamed on the wrong cause. A real fix needs the Phase 2 backend.

Anti-bot defences will break it, repeatedly

A competitor is publicly running with its AMC alerts disabled for lack of data. An open-source Ticketmaster monitor is dead from Imperva tokens. This will happen to VIGIL; the question is whether re-recording is fast enough to absorb it.

Basic polling is worth nothing

Maintained open-source Discord monitors for Shopify, SNKRS, Supreme and Footsites are free on GitHub; freelancers build custom ones for ~$30. Defensibility cannot come from “we poll a URL.” It has to come from the catalogue, the shared templates, and the correctness work.

What to do

Ranked, and honestly scoped

  1. Get it onto one real booking page Everything else is speculation until this happens. One European cinema, one real recording, one real alert.
  2. Lead with Europe Unserved, documented pain, and the catalogue already covers it.
  3. Make the refusal to buy verifiable, not just stated The rhetorical foil writes itself: one competing service has members submit shipping, billing and payment details to a Discord server so staff can check out for them. That's where “just a little automation” ends up.
  4. Price-range alerts, and an inline price sparkline Both small. The range rule is the most-copied feature in ticket resale; the sparkline is presentation over data already stored.
  5. Format-targeted “new showtime added at this venue” The least-served trigger found anywhere, and technically tractable — several chains expose format as a query parameter.

Deliberately don't

  • No auto-checkout, in any partial form. Not pre-filled carts, not “assisted” checkout, not auto-opening the listing. The line is worth more intact than any conversion it buys.
  • No ML drop-prediction claims. Unclaimed by everyone credible — possibly because it doesn't work. The search results for it are already polluted with AI-generated fake products; don't join them.
  • Don't artificially delay free users to upsell speed, the way one competitor does. And say so.
